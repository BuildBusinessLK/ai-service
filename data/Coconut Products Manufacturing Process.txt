TABLE 1: MANUFACTURING PROCESSES
COCONUT OIL (Virgin & Refined)
Virgin Coconut Oil (VCO) - Cold Press Method
Step 1: Coconut Selection & Dehusking
- Duration: 2 hours per 100 coconuts
- Equipment: Dehusking tools, manual or mechanical
- Labor: 2-3 workers (unskilled)
- Quality Standard: Select mature brown coconuts (11-12 months)
- Input: 100 coconuts → Output: 60kg copra equivalent
- Cost per step: $15-20


Step 2: Splitting & Meat Extraction
- Duration: 3 hours per 100 coconuts
- Equipment: Splitting knives, meat extractors
- Labor: 3-4 workers (semi-skilled)
- Quality Standard: Clean white meat, no mold
- Input: 100 coconuts → Output: 35-40kg fresh meat
- Cost per step: $20-25


Step 3: Grating/Grinding
- Duration: 1 hour
- Equipment: Coconut grater/grinder (electric)
- Power: 2-3 kW
- Labor: 1 operator (skilled)
- Input: 35kg meat → Output: 35kg grated coconut
- Cost per step: $5-8


Step 4: Pressing (Cold Press)
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Equipment: Hydraulic/Screw expeller press
- Temperature: Below 40°C (cold press requirement)
- Labor: 2 operators (skilled)
- Yield: 65-70% oil extraction
- Input: 35kg grated → Output: 23-25L oil
- Cost per step: $30-40


Step 5: Settling & Filtering
- Duration: 24-48 hours settling + 2 hours filtering
- Equipment: Settling tanks, filter press/cloth filters
- Labor: 1 worker (semi-skilled)
- Quality Standard: Clear, no sediment
- Loss: 2-3%
- Cost per step: $10-15


Step 6: Bottling & Packaging
- Duration: 3-4 hours per 100 bottles
- Equipment: Filling machine, capping machine, labeling
- Labor: 3-4 workers
- Quality Standard: Food-grade bottles, airtight seal
- Cost per step: $25-35
Refined Coconut Oil (RBD)
Additional Steps After Extraction:
Step 7: Refining (Neutralization)
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Equipment: Refining tanks, caustic soda treatment
- Temperature: 60-70°C
- Removes: Free fatty acids
- Cost: $40-50 per batch


Step 8: Bleaching
- Duration: 2-3 hours
- Equipment: Bleaching tanks, activated carbon/clay
- Temperature: 90-110°C
- Removes: Color pigments, impurities
- Cost: $30-40 per batch


Step 9: Deodorization
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Equipment: Deodorizer, steam distillation
- Temperature: 180-220°C under vacuum
- Removes: Odor compounds
- Cost: $50-70 per batch

TODDY (Fresh & Fermented)
Fresh Toddy Collection
Step 1: Tree Preparation
- Duration: 30 minutes per tree
- Equipment: Climbing equipment, tapping knife
- Labor: 1 skilled tapper
- Quality: Select healthy, mature palms (15+ years)
- Frequency: Daily tapping
- Yield: 2-5 liters per tree per day
- Cost: $2-3 per tree per day


Step 2: Inflorescence Tapping
- Duration: 15-20 minutes per tree
- Technique: Slice thin layer from flower spadix
- Tools: Sharp curved knife (sanitized)
- Collection vessel: Clay pot or food-grade plastic
- Best time: Early morning (4-6 AM)
- Cost: Labor included in Step 1


Step 3: Collection & Transportation
- Duration: 2-3 hours for 50 trees
- Collection: Every 12 hours (morning/evening)
- Container: Food-grade containers
- Temperature control: Keep cool (below 20°C)
- Shelf life: 6-8 hours fresh
- Cost: $10-15 per collection round


Step 4: Filtering & Quality Check
- Duration: 30 minutes per 50L
- Equipment: Fine cloth/mesh filters
- Quality checks: Color (whitish), smell (sweet), no fermentation
- pH level: 7.0-7.5 (fresh)
- Storage: Refrigerated if not immediate use
- Cost: $5-8

COIR FIBER
Traditional Coir Processing
Step 1: Husk Collection
- Duration: Continuous
- Source: Coconut processing units
- Storage: Covered area, prevent moisture loss
- Quality: Fresh husks preferred (within 7 days)
- Input ratio: 1000 coconuts = 600kg husks
- Cost: $0.05-0.10 per husk


Step 2: Retting (Fermentation)
- Duration: 6-12 months (traditional) OR 10-15 days (mechanical)
- Method: 
  * Traditional: Submerge in lagoons/pits
  * Modern: Bacterial retting tanks
- Equipment: Retting pits (10m x 5m x 2m)
- Labor: 2-3 workers for loading/unloading
- Output: Softened husks, fiber separation ready
- Cost: $50-100 per ton


Step 3: Defibering/Beating
- Duration: 8 hours per ton
- Equipment: 
  * Manual: Wooden mallets
  * Mechanical: Decorticator machine
- Labor: 4-5 workers (manual) OR 2 operators (mechanical)
- Output: 30-35% fiber yield from husks
- Quality: Brown fiber (longer, stronger)
- Cost: $80-120 per ton


Step 4: Washing & Cleaning
- Duration: 4 hours per ton
- Equipment: Washing drums, water supply
- Process: Remove pith, dust, short fibers
- Water usage: 2000-3000L per ton
- Quality standard: Clean, no impurities
- Cost: $30-40 per ton


Step 5: Drying
- Duration: 48-72 hours (sun drying)
- Space required: 100 sq.m per ton
- Equipment: Drying yards, turning tools
- Labor: 2 workers for turning/monitoring
- Final moisture: 12-15%
- Cost: $20-30 per ton


Step 6: Grading & Baling
- Duration: 6 hours per ton
- Equipment: Baling press (hydraulic)
- Grades: 
  * Long fiber (25cm+): Premium
  * Medium (15-25cm): Standard
  * Short (<15cm): Low grade
- Bale weight: 100-120 kg
- Cost: $40-50 per ton

JAGGERY (Coconut Palm)
Traditional Jaggery Making
Step 1: Toddy Collection for Jaggery
- Duration: 4-5 hours (morning collection)
- Volume: 50-100 liters per batch
- Quality: Fresh toddy, not fermented
- pH: 7.0-7.5
- Storage: Immediate processing required
- Cost: $25-40 per 100L


Step 2: Filtering & Cleaning
- Duration: 30 minutes
- Equipment: Fine cloth filters
- Process: Remove debris, insects, impurities
- Loss: 2-3%
- Cost: $5


Step 3: Boiling & Concentration
- Duration: 4-8 hours (depending on volume)
- Equipment: 
  * Large iron/brass vessel (200L capacity)
  * Firewood hearth
- Fuel: 40-50kg firewood per 100L toddy
- Temperature: 100-110°C, constant stirring
- Evaporation: 80-85% water removal
- Output: 15-20kg jaggery per 100L toddy
- Labor: 2 skilled workers (constant monitoring)
- Cost: $30-50 per batch


Step 4: Testing for Doneness
- Duration: Continuous during boiling
- Tests:
  * Thread test: Forms hard thread between fingers
  * Water test: Drops form hard ball in cold water
  * Color: Golden brown to dark brown
- Skill level: High (experience required)


Step 5: Cooling & Molding
- Duration: 2-4 hours
- Equipment: 
  * Wooden/metal molds (various shapes)
  * Coconut oil for greasing
- Process: Pour thick syrup into molds
- Cooling: Natural air cooling
- Output: Solid blocks/cakes
- Cost: $10-15


Step 6: Packaging
- Duration: 2 hours per 50kg
- Materials: Food-grade plastic wrap or paper
- Labeling: Weight, date, ingredients
- Shelf life: 6-12 months (cool, dry storage)
- Cost: $8-12

COCONUT VINEGAR
Fermentation Process
Step 1: Toddy Collection for Vinegar
- Duration: Same as fresh toddy collection
- Volume: 100-200L per batch
- Quality: Can use slightly fermented toddy
- Storage: Large fermentation vessels
- Cost: $30-50 per 100L


Step 2: Natural Fermentation (Alcoholic Phase)
- Duration: 20-30 days
- Equipment: Food-grade plastic/ceramic vessels (100-200L)
- Temperature: 25-30°C (ambient)
- Process: Natural yeasts convert sugars to alcohol
- Alcohol content: 4-6%
- Monitoring: Check smell, taste daily
- Labor: 1 worker for daily checks
- Cost: $15-20 (monitoring + utilities)


Step 3: Acetic Acid Fermentation
- Duration: 30-60 days
- Equipment: Same vessels with cloth cover (air access)
- Process: Acetobacter bacteria convert alcohol to acetic acid
- Temperature: 25-32°C
- Acidity target: 4-6% acetic acid
- Monitoring: pH testing, taste
- Cost: $20-30


Step 4: Filtering & Clarification
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Equipment: 
  * Filter press or cloth filters
  * Settling tanks
- Process: Remove sediment, mother of vinegar
- Clarity: Clear to slightly cloudy
- Cost: $15-20


Step 5: Pasteurization (Optional)
- Duration: 2 hours
- Equipment: Heating vessel with temperature control
- Temperature: 65-70°C for 30 minutes
- Purpose: Stop fermentation, extend shelf life
- Cost: $20-25


Step 6: Bottling
- Duration: 3-4 hours per 100L
- Equipment: Filling machine, bottles, caps
- Quality: Food-grade glass bottles preferred
- Labeling: Acidity %, ingredients, date
- Shelf life: 2 years (sealed)
- Cost: $30-40

STORAGE & INVENTORY MANAGEMENT
Raw Material Storage
Coconuts (Fresh):
- Storage duration: 7-10 days maximum
- Facility: Covered, ventilated shed
- Capacity: 500-1,000 nuts per 100 sq.ft
- Handling: Avoid dropping (causes cracks)
- Loss rate: 2-3% (natural drying, damage)
- Inventory policy: Just-in-time (weekly purchases)


Copra:
- Storage duration: 2-3 months
- Facility: Dry, moisture-free warehouse
- Packaging: Jute bags (50kg each)
- Stack height: Maximum 10 bags
- Loss rate: 1-2% (moisture, pests)
- Inventory: 1-month buffer stock


Husks:
- Fresh husks: Use within 7 days (for quality fiber)
- Dried husks: Can store 3-6 months
- Storage: Covered area, off ground
- Space: 1 ton requires 15-20 cubic meters
- Loss: 5-7% (decomposition if wet)


Toddy:
- Storage: Refrigerated (below 10°C)
- Duration: 6-8 hours (fresh)
- Container: Food-grade plastic/stainless steel
- Inventory: Daily purchase, daily use (for jaggery)
- For vinegar: Immediate transfer to fermentation


Finished Products:
Virgin Coconut Oil:
- Storage: Cool, dark place (below 25°C)
- Container: Stainless steel drums or glass
- Duration: 12-18 months
- Inventory: 2-week stock + buffer


Jaggery:
- Storage: Cool, dry, airtight containers
- Duration: 6-12 months
- Packaging: Vacuum sealed preferred
- Inventory: 1-month stock


Coir Products:
- Storage: Dry warehouse
- Duration: Unlimited (if dry)
- Space: 1 ton compressed = 3-4 cubic meters
- Inventory: 1-2 month stock
Inventory Optimization
Economic Order Quantity (EOQ):


For Coconuts (example):
- Annual requirement: 180,000 nuts
- Ordering cost: $50 per order (transport, admin)
- Holding cost: $0.02 per nut per month


EOQ = √(2 × Annual Demand × Ordering Cost / Holding Cost)
EOQ = √(2 × 180,000 × 50 / (0.02 × 12))
EOQ ≈ 8,660 nuts per order


Optimal: Order ~9,000 nuts every 2 weeks


Safety Stock:
- Lead time: 2-3 days
- Daily usage: 600 nuts
- Safety stock: 1,800-2,000 nuts
- Reorder point: (Daily usage × Lead time) + Safety stock
- Reorder at: 3,800-4,000 nuts remaining
